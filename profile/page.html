Hello

If we've just spoken, good catching you. If we haven't, come and say hello.

In 2024 I put a little Meshtastic node on the windowsill to see how far it would reach, got completely hooked on the idea of things talking to each other over the air, and sat my Foundation licence early in 2025. DMR came first, because that's where the local folk were. Then 2m FM, which is still my favourite way to spend twenty minutes. HF is my latest adventure.

I write software for a living, so most of my hobbies end up with me building a tool for the hobby. I'm far more interested in the tinkering and talking to people, than chasing DX and FT8. If you want to talk about antennas in an attic, mesh nodes in trees, or why your codeplug won't import, I'm your man.

Mesh, and how it started

I've been part of the Central Belt Scotland Meshtastic community since 2024, and it has grown well beyond anything I expected - we hit coast-to-coast coverage in late 2025 and it keeps creeping north. My nodes all begin with PDY; PDY4 is the one that's usually awake. If you see it pop up on your map, that's me.

More recently I've been running MeshCore alongside Meshtastic to see how the two compare. Both are far more fun with neighbours, so if you're anywhere near Glasgow and fancy putting a node up, I'm happy to help you get going.

On the bands

DMR is what got me licensed. There's nearly always a DMR handheld in a pocket or on the desk, and with GB7GL covering most of the west of Scotland I end up listening to the Scottish talkgroups more than anything else.

HF is the newer adventure. The antenna situation is very much a work in progress: 22m of wire across the garden into a 9:1 unun gives me receive from 80m to 10m and reasonable transmit from 40m up. A 6m dipole and a 20m Moxon are waiting on a mounting puzzle I haven't solved yet. Patience with my operating is appreciated and usually repaid with enthusiasm.

Things I've made

I love building - electronics and the physical side both. The one I'm proudest of is a portable DMR hotspot: off-the-shelf internals in a tidy enclosure, battery powered, USB charging, and it simply works, which is rarer than it sounds.

Beyond that it's mostly mesh nodes: solar-powered WisBlocks, 3D-printed Heltec V3 cases, and a few tiny Seeed Xiao nodes living inside scavenged vape pens, which feels like the correct fate for a vape pen. For everyday use I usually reach for the Seeed Tracker.
